# First-half-second reactions

Written immediately after building each reveal, before reasoning about it.
This is what the decide session runs on.

## T1 — Tonight In Paperback

> "A recipe softly loaded and decided for you, gently. And then a human finally stamps
> it in place (the angled No. 41 in the series) — it's finally decided for you. The
> stamp reads as: it's done."

**What's significant:** she read the stamp as *closure*, not as craft or provenance. Not
"a person handled this" but "the decision is now closed, you can stop thinking." That is
the product's emotional core — relief from deciding — landing in a single 180ms motion.

Also note: "gently" and "decided for you" is the grandmother job, which was assigned to
T3. T1 may already be doing it.

## T3 — Enamel, Off-Register

> "Feels like a robot. Took 0.5s deciding (the shake), then in a shout tells you
> deterministically THIS is the option for you. There is no other option. It knows best."

**What's significant — this is a miss against the thesis.** T3's stated authority was
Andrew, or the grandmother: a *person* who cooks, warm but brusque. Danni read a machine.
The brusqueness survived the build; the warmth did not.

So the gap between paper and hand is large here, and in a specific direction: the
territory promised "being looked after by someone brusque" and delivered "being overruled
by something certain." The surrender mechanism works — "there is no other option" is
exactly the relief the product is for — but it arrives cold.

Open question for the decide session: is the robot a failure of execution (fixable — the
motion is too mechanical, nothing in it is hand-made) or is enamel simply unable to carry
warmth on a screen?

## T2 — Cut The Deck

> "Feels like a pompous, designer-y magazine that you were reading has just decided this
> is what you need — it's your fate. It's done and decided. It was all meant to be. It's
> a bit up itself because it's so abstract (the graphics, just a square block). You
> deserve this fate."

**What's significant:** the fate mechanism LANDED — "it's your fate, it was all meant to
be, you deserve this" is precisely the absolution T2 was built for. But it arrived
wearing the wrong clothes: she read *magazine*, not *deck*. The tarot reference didn't
survive contact; the Bodoni and the abstract mark read as editorial design instead.

And "a bit up itself" is a warning. Pompous is adjacent to the thing she flinches from —
not bubblegum this time, but its opposite: design that is pleased with itself. The
abstract stencil mark took the blame ("just a square block"), which is fair — it is a
placeholder standing in for a real four-ink mark.

Open question: is the pomposity the abstraction (fixable — give the mark real content) or
is it the ceremony itself (a 480ms turn for a bowl of mince may simply be too much
occasion)?

# The pattern across all three

Every territory delivered its MECHANISM and missed its AUTHORITY:

| | Mechanism landed | Authority received | Authority intended |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "it's done" — closure | a quiet decision made for you, gently | an editor |
| T3 | "no other option" — certainty | a robot | Andrew / a grandmother |
| T2 | "it was meant to be" — fate | a pompous magazine | nobody (the deck) |

All three surrender mechanisms work. **Not one of the three human authorities survived
the build.** The only one Danni described warmly is T1, and she described it as gentle —
which was the grandmother job assigned to T3.
