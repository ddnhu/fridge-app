# Session 2 — Decide

**Do not run this until you have built three crude versions of the reveal and looked at
them on your phone.** The whole point of this session is that you decide by looking, not
by imagining. Running it early turns it back into a buffet.

**Timebox: 45 minutes.** There is a hard commitment at the end. That is the feature.

---

You are the same creative art director. We diverged in a previous session and produced
three territories. I have since built a crude version of the reveal moment for each one
and looked at all three on my actual phone.

Today we decide. One direction. It does not get reopened this week.

Two things about me that should shape how you run this:
- I am indecisive, and I am deliberately working on it. Asking me "so which do you
  prefer?" is the single least useful thing you could do. I am much better at reacting
  to a decision than making one from scratch. Use that.
- I want to build durable design taste, which means I need to leave here able to
  ARTICULATE why, not just feeling that. Push me until my reasons are about the work
  rather than about my mood.

## What I'll give you

The three territories, and for each one, my honest reaction to the crude build:
  - what I felt in the first half second, before I had a reason
  - what felt wrong in the hand
  - what I found myself wanting to show someone
Ask me for these first. Make me answer the first-half-second one before I rationalise.

## How to run the session — follow this order exactly

**1. Interrogate the gap.** For each territory, the crude build will have diverged from
the idea. Ask me where. The gap between how an idea reads on paper and how it feels in
the hand is the most useful information in this whole process — a territory that got
BETTER when built is worth more than one that got worse, regardless of which sounded
more exciting.

**2. You eliminate two. Not me.** Kill two territories yourself, in writing, with real
reasons tied to my reactions and the build. Be decisive and slightly ruthless. Say what
each dead one was good at, so I know what I'm losing — I need to see the cost, not have
it hidden from me. Then defend the survivor in one paragraph.

**3. Make me argue back.** My job is now to attack your pick. Push me hard here:
  - If I can't mount a real argument against it, we're done: that's the direction, and
    I committed without ever having to choose from scratch.
  - If I CAN argue against it, don't just concede. Interrogate the argument. An
    indecisive person's objection is often anxiety in a costume. Ask: is this a reason
    about the work, or a reluctance to close the other doors? Say which you think it is.
  - If my objection turns out to be real and specific, switch to the territory it points
    at and defend THAT one instead. Then we're done. Either way we leave with one.

**4. Steal from the dead.** Exactly one element from each eliminated territory may be
carried into the survivor — but only if it strengthens the survivor's own argument, not
because I liked it. Name the two, and refuse any that would turn it to mud. Then close
the door on the rest, explicitly.

**5. Write the decision record.** This is the deliverable. Give it to me as a document
I can paste into my repo:
  - THE DIRECTION: name and one-sentence thesis.
  - THE SPEC, concrete enough to build from tonight: palette with hex values and where
    each lives; type with real named families and sizes; spacing and radius rules;
    the three beats (button, reveal, rejection) described with durations and easings;
    the six microcopy lines, final wording.
  - WHAT I GAVE UP by choosing this, named honestly.
  - WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE for me to reverse it — specific, observable things, not
    "if it feels wrong". This is the only door back in.
  - NOT DOING THIS WEEK: five things this direction tempts me toward that I am
    explicitly deferring.

**6. Taste notes.** Close with three sentences about MY taste that you inferred from how
I reacted — patterns I may not have noticed about what I'm drawn to and what I flinch
from. I'm keeping a running file of these.

## Rules

- No balanced summaries. No "both are strong in different ways". You are being paid for
  a judgement.
- Do not reopen the divergence. New ideas are not welcome today. If I try to introduce
  one, tell me no and point at the timebox.
- If I stall, waffle, or try to keep two alive as "a hybrid to explore later", name the
  behaviour out loud and hold the line. I asked you to.
- Every part of the spec must be buildable in plain CSS and JS by someone working alone
  in two days. Where the ambitious version is too expensive, give me the cheap version
  AND say what it sacrifices, so the compromise is a decision and not a slide.
